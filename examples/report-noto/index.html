Cooling a sealed enclosure

Technical note 7 · a lumped model, sixty-one readings, and how well one explains the other

A sealed aluminium enclosure was heated to a uniform temperature and left to cool in still air. This note compares its temperature with the simplest model that could describe it, Newton’s law of cooling , whose one parameter, the time constant τ, can be predicted from the enclosure’s mass and surface or fitted to the readings . 1. Model

If the enclosure’s temperature T is uniform — its walls conduct heat far faster than air carries it away — the rate at which it cools is proportional to its excess over the ambient temperature Ta:

dTdt =− T−Taτ

Starting from T0 at t=0, its solution is an exponential approach to the ambient temperature:

T(t)= Ta+ (T0−Ta) e−t/τ

An energy balance gives the time constant from the enclosure itself: the heat it stores, mc, over the rate at which its surface A gives heat to the air through the film coefficient h,The film coefficient for still air over a vertical plate is between about 5 and 25 W m−2 K−1; 12 is the value this enclosure’s datasheet quotes, and the one used here.

The model holds while the Biot number Bi=hL/k is small — while the walls are thin or conductive enough that their inside and outside stay at one temperature. With walls 3 mm thick and aluminium’s conductivity it is about 2×10−4, far below the usual limit of 0.1.

4. Conclusion

A single exponential with a time constant computed from the datasheet describes an hour of cooling to within a few tenths of a kelvin, and fitting that one number moves it by only a few percent.

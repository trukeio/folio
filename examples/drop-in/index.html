Auroræ

Their characters and spectra

The aurora is a phenomenon of the upper air, and the observations which follow were made with instruments of no great power, in a climate where clear nights are the exception rather than the rule. They are offered less as measurements than as a record of what was seen, and of the manner of seeing itInstrument and method are described in the second chapter..

Of the colours, green predominates, and after it a red which is rarely pure. The spectroscope resolves the first into a single bright line whose position has been the subject of much dispute; the second is fainter, more diffuse, and appears chiefly at the upper margin of the display, where the streamers lose their definition against the dark of the sky.

The light is not of the kind that falls upon a landscape; it is of the kind that a landscape is discovered to be standing in.

Successive nights were seldom alike. On the eleventh the arc stood almost motionless for above an hour, its lower edge as sharp as a drawn line; on the twelfth nothing appeared at all, though the conditions were to every appearance the same. It is this irregularity, more than any difficulty of observation, which makes the subject a hard one to reduce to order.

The method

The instrument was a direct-vision spectroscope of five prisms, mounted so that it could be swung to any part of the sky without disturbing the illumination of the scale. Readings were taken to the nearest half division and afterwards reduced; the reduction is given in the table, and the raw figures are kept in the notebooks.

Where the display was bright enough to be examined at leisure, the line was compared directly with a reference flame. Where it was not — and this was the more common case — the setting was made as quickly as the eye would allow, and repeated as often as the aurora lasted. The agreement between such hurried settings is better than might be feared.

The remainder of this chapter is given to the individual displays, in the order in which they were seen. Nothing has been omitted on the ground of being unremarkable, since it is by the unremarkable nights that the remarkable ones are to be judged.
